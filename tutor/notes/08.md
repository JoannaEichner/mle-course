## What the learner should own after this module

- A model is a function with parameters; the loss measures misfit; training minimises the loss on training data; the goal is low loss on future data.
- Gradient descent: move parameters against the gradient; the step size decides between crawling, converging and diverging; standardise features first.
- Train / validation / test split by time (C3). Decisions on validation, test once at the end (C5).
- Leakage: outcome columns of the same day, lags shorter than the horizon, preprocessing fitted on everything (C2, C4). A result too good to be true is a symptom.
- Baseline on the same rows (C6); seasonal naive for daily sales.
- Regularisation (Ridge `alpha`) and under/over-fitting; `alpha` is a hyperparameter chosen on validation.

Assumed: metrics and masks (05), the processed panel and `add_lag`/`add_rolling_mean` (07).

## Facts worth having at hand

Standard profile (city 3), processed panel 238,320 rows, 2024-03-28 to 2024-06-25.

- Split: train 201,248 rows (to 2024-06-11), validation 18,536 (06-12 to 06-18), test 18,536 (06-19 to 06-25).
- The 8 given features need 34 days of history (28-day mean ending 7 days back), so 111,216 of 201,248 training rows have all features. Test and validation rows have them all.
- Validation: seasonal naive WAPE 0.620 all / 0.558 in-stock (bias in-stock −0.118); Ridge 0.502 / 0.443 (bias −0.106).
- Gradient descent on the standardised features: steps 0.003-0.3 converge (0.3 fastest); 1.5 diverges and raises; around 0.5 the loss explodes to about 1e157 without becoming infinite within 300 steps. Weights match `LinearRegression` to 3 decimals after 500 steps at 0.1. The largest weights: 28-day mean 0.162, discount −0.150, lag 7 0.103.
- The "all columns" model includes `sales_in_stock` and `sales_while_oos`, which add up to the target: WAPE 0.0000 on validation. Its weights: `sales_in_stock` 0.617, `sales_while_oos` 0.061, everything else 0.000. The design doc's 0.034 was measured with `sales_in_stock` alone.
- Adding only `oos_hours` (same day): WAPE all days 0.465 (better than 0.502), in-stock 0.459 (worse than 0.443).
- `alpha` from 0.01 to 1,000 gives the same in-stock WAPE (0.4429-0.4430); 10,000 gives 0.4446, 100,000 0.4631. The notebook picks 0.01.
- Test (fit on train + validation): seasonal naive 0.586 all / 0.548 in-stock (bias in-stock −0.032); Ridge 0.490 / 0.447 (bias in-stock −0.074). Ridge 0.490 matches the design doc.

Small profile (city 14), processed panel 44,370 rows, same dates, 493 series of 90 days:

- Split: train 37,468, validation 3,451, test 3,451. Training rows with all 8 features: 20,706.
- Validation: seasonal naive WAPE 0.426 all / 0.378 in-stock; Ridge (`alpha=1`) 0.368 / 0.328.
- After 500 GD steps at 0.1 the weights are not yet at the minimum: they differ from `LinearRegression` by up to 0.075 (the rolling means), while the MSE is 0.6963 against 0.6961. The lab text says this; the assert compares losses, not weights.
- "All columns": WAPE 0.0001; largest weights `sales_in_stock` 2.159, `sales_while_oos` 0.120. With `oos_hours`: 0.358 all (better than 0.368), 0.333 in-stock (worse than 0.328).
- `alpha` matters more here: in-stock WAPE 0.3276 at 0.01 and 1, 0.3248 at 100, 0.3174 at 1,000 (picked), 0.3308 at 10,000, 0.4809 at 100,000. Question 2 of the lab therefore asks how much `alpha` changes the result on the learner's profile.
- Test: seasonal naive 0.404 / 0.336 (bias in-stock −0.009); Ridge 0.338 / 0.274 (bias in-stock −0.029).

## Tasks

### 08.1 `last_days_start`, `split_by_date`

- Off by one: `last - Timedelta(days=days)` gives 8 days. The check names the expected date.
- Returning `reset_index` frames: the docstring says keep the index; predictions are aligned by it later.
- No error for a cutoff outside the data: the check passes one.

### 08.2 `PerSeriesForecaster`

- Forecasts returned in groupby order instead of the order of `X`. The check shuffles rows; ask what position the first row of `X` has in their output.
- Not sorting the history by date in `fit`. Ask what `history[-7:]` means for an unsorted series.
- Reusing one local forecaster for all series: works for these stateless-after-fit classes, but ask what `_make_local` is for.
- Unknown series in `predict`: the check expects `ValueError`.
- Keys: `groupby(SERIES_KEY)` gives tuple keys; `groupby("store_id")` alone merges products.

### 08.3 Gradient descent

- Gradient sign or factor: the check compares with a finite-difference estimate. Ask them to compute the gradient of `mean(r**2)` for one weight by hand.
- `losses` recorded after the update instead of before: `losses[0]` must be the all-zero model's loss.
- Forgetting the divergence check: the check uses a huge learning rate and expects `ValueError`.
- Standardising with validation statistics or on all rows (C4): the notebook does it on the fit rows; if they change it, ask which rule it breaks.

### 08.4 `LinearForecaster`

- Filling NaN inside the model (`fillna(0)`): the docstring says raise. Ask what a zero lag would claim.
- Fitting the scaler once in `__init__` or on `X` in `predict`: leakage and wrong scaling. `make_pipeline` in `fit` is the pattern.
- Not clipping: the check feeds data that gives a negative prediction.

### 08.5 `score_forecast`

- Keys in a different order or names: the check lists the expected keys.
- Computing in-stock metrics by filtering one array only. `mask=` in the metrics from 05 is the tool.

## 08.6 and 08.7: the trap and the final table

- If the learner celebrates the near-zero WAPE, do not tell them. Ask: "What WAPE would a perfect forecaster of next week's fresh-food sales have? Is 0.0000 plausible?" Then: "Which weight is largest?"
- The rule to write: a feature must be known at forecast time; columns known only after the day (`OUTCOME_COLUMNS`) never enter a model of that day. Rule C2.
- `discount_filled` is not a reversal of lab 07's "no `fillna(1.0)`" (07.2b): the stored file keeps NaN; the filled copy exists only as model input, next to the flag. The lab says so under "Cechy znane tydzień wcześniej".
- Questions 3 and 4 now ask the learner to read their own numbers first; on both profiles the directions match the notes (with `oos_hours`: all-days WAPE better, in-stock worse; Ridge in-stock bias negative on test).
- If they want to "fix" the model by dropping only `sales_in_stock`, ask about `sales_while_oos` and `oos_hours`: same day, same problem.

## The questions at the end of the lab

Discuss, do not grade.

1. **Why a time split.** A random split puts days after the validation days into training; neighbouring days of one series are similar, so the model effectively sees the answer. Validation WAPE would be too optimistic compared with a real forecast.
2. **How much `alpha` matters.** Standard profile: 8 features, 111,216 rows, least squares is already stable and `alpha` barely matters. Small profile: about five times fewer rows and a visible optimum at 1,000. Regularisation matters with many features relative to rows, correlated features, or noisy targets with few rows.
3. **`oos_hours` better on all days, worse in-stock.** The feature explains part of the recorded sales on stockout days, so the all-days WAPE improves. On in-stock days it pushes forecasts up: in-stock bias moves from −0.106 to +0.040 and in-stock WAPE gets worse (0.443 to 0.459). Its relation to sales is not linear either (module 07: sales peak at two to three stockout hours, because popular products sell out). The point that matters most: the feature is unknown at forecast time, so the comparison is academic.
4. **Negative in-stock bias.** The model forecasts too little on days without stockouts: it learned partly from stockout days, whose sales are below demand. For a shop that means ordering too little. Module 09 trains on in-stock days only.
