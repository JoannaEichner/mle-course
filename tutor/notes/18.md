# Module 18: neural forecasting on the GPU

Lecture: `lectures/18-neural-gpu/index.html`. Lab: `labs/18-neural-gpu/` (tickets in `README.md`; the notebook checks the hardware, shows the inputs, trains on the last fold, compares GPU and CPU, backtests GRU against LightGBM and repeats the training in Lightning). Code: `src/freshcast/neural/` (`device.py`, `windows.py`, `gru.py`, `training.py`, `lightning.py`) and `src/freshcast/models/neural.py`. Needs `uv sync --group neural`.

The only module that expects an NVIDIA GPU. Everything runs on the CPU too, about 15-20 times slower per epoch.

## What the learner should own after this module

- A global model: one network for all series, trained on windows (28 days of history, 7 to forecast), with the past target scaled by the window's own mean.
- Devices: model and batch on the same device, one transfer per batch, out-of-memory solved by a smaller batch first.
- `Dataset` / `DataLoader`, and building a whole batch at once with array indexing (`__getitems__`).
- GRU as a learned recurrence with gates; embeddings as learned vectors per store and product.
- A masked loss: stockout days stay in the input, leave the loss.
- Mixed precision (autocast + GradScaler) and why float16 needs the scaler; one-cycle learning rate; early stopping with a best-weights copy, then a refit on everything for the chosen number of epochs.
- Lightning: what it takes over (loop, device, precision, early stopping, checkpoints) and when a plain loop is better.

## Facts worth having at hand

Standard profile (city 3), RTX 4070 Ti SUPER on the author's machine.

- Cube: (2,648 series, 90 days, 10 channels). Past channels: sales, discount, zero-discount flag, activity, holiday, day-of-week sin/cos, stockout hours, temperature, precipitation. Future channels: the same without sales and stockout hours (8). The channels include features from the catalogue, so the cube is built from `catalog.build_feature_table(daily)`, not from the processed panel.
- Windows per series: 56 (90 − 35 + 1); 148,288 in the panel.
- `GRUForecastNet` with 101 stores and 173 products: 115,495 parameters.
- One `GRUForecaster.fit` on the last fold: about 5-6 s on the GPU (10 epochs of about 0.25 s in the early-stopping run, best epoch 7, plus the refit); one CPU epoch about 4.3 s. Last-fold in-stock WAPE 0.379, bias −0.064.
- Backtest, 4 folds, in-stock WAPE: LightGBM 0.395, 0.394, 0.358, 0.361 (mean 0.377, bias +0.038, 2.8 s); GRU 0.407, 0.393, 0.382, 0.379 (mean 0.390, bias −0.046, 12.5 s). The GRU trains on all days with the masked loss; LightGBM on in-stock days.
- Full profile (design doc, author's machine): GRU 0.308, LightGBM 0.306.
- Lightning on the same windows: best validation loss 0.2978 against 0.2970 for the plain loop, about 2.4 s. Lightning prints a banner and deprecation warnings; the notebook silences them.

## Tickets

### 18.1 device and seed

- `torch.cuda.is_available()` False on WSL: the NVIDIA driver belongs in Windows; `nvidia-smi` inside WSL must work first. The course's PyTorch wheel carries its own CUDA libraries; no CUDA toolkit installation is needed.
- Seeding only NumPy or only PyTorch: the check reseeds and compares both.

### 18.2 `panel_cube`

- Sorting inside the function: the check wants an error for an unsorted panel (sorting silently would misalign the result with the caller's frame).
- Missing days: the reshape then mixes series. The check has a panel with a gap.
- `keys` built with `drop_duplicates` on an unsorted frame: order differs from the cube.

### 18.3 `WindowDataset`

- Building examples one by one in `__getitems__`: correct and slow; the lab's loaders expect batches. Ask them to time 1,000 windows both ways.
- Scaling the target channel of the future days: the target stays in original units, only the past is scaled.
- Off by one in the future days (starting at `start + past - 1`).
- Scale of zero for a series without sales in the window: the reference floors it at 0.1.

### 18.4 `GRUForecastNet`

- Using the GRU output sequence instead of the last hidden state.
- Forgetting the embeddings or the future channels in the head: the check changes the store code and the future channels and expects the forecast to change.
- Output `(batch, horizon, 1)` instead of `(batch, horizon)`.

### 18.5 training

- `masked_mae` over all days, or NaN targets propagating: the check has both.
- Autocast without GradScaler on float16: training works for a while, then losses go flat or NaN. Ask what `np.float16(1e-4) ** 2` is.
- Best weights saved by reference (`net.state_dict()` without a copy): the "best" follows the model, as in module 13.
- Validation without `torch.no_grad()` / `net.eval()`: slower, more memory, and wrong with dropout.
- Forgetting to move a tensor to the device: "Expected all tensors to be on the same device". Ask which tensor lives where (`tensor.device`).

### 18.6 `GRUForecaster`

- Training only once, with the last week held out: the most recent week is lost to validation. The docstring asks for the refit.
- `predict` with dates that are not exactly the next `horizon` days: the check expects an error.

### 18.7 Lightning

- Codes as plain attributes instead of buffers: they stay on the CPU while the network moves to the GPU.
- Returning the last weights instead of loading the best checkpoint.
- `precision="16-mixed"` on the CPU: the reference uses 32-bit on the CPU.

## The questions at the end of the lab

Discuss, do not grade.

1. **GPU speed-up.** About 17 times per epoch (4.3 s against 0.25 s). With 50,000 series the windows grow about 19 times; on the GPU an epoch stays in seconds, on the CPU it becomes more than a minute per epoch, which is why the full profile needs the card.
2. **GRU against LightGBM.** LightGBM wins by 0.013 on one city; the fold-to-fold spread is larger (0.36-0.40), but LightGBM wins three folds clearly and the second is a tie (0.394 against 0.393), so the difference is consistent rather than noise. On the full data the gap nearly closes (0.308 against 0.306).
3. **Stockout days in the GRU.** They are inputs (the history must be continuous, and the stockout hours are a channel), but the loss ignores them as targets, so the network is not taught that capped sales are demand.
4. **Lightning.** Gives: less code, standard callbacks, checkpoints, precision and device handling. Takes: control and transparency; unusual training needs its hooks. A plain loop is better for learning and for non-standard training.
