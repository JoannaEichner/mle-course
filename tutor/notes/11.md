## What the learner should own after this module

- A rolling-origin backtest replays production: train on everything before a window, score the window, several windows. The spread between folds is the noise floor.
- A random K-fold on time series leaks the future through neighbouring days and flatters the model.
- Compare models fold by fold, not only by the mean.
- The optimised metric follows the decision; bias matters when error costs are asymmetric.
- Search strategies (grid, random, TPE) and why the best trial's score is optimistic (selection among noisy measurements); the test is looked at once.
- Experiment tracking: runs with parameters, metrics, artifacts; or a directory with metadata. What reproduction needs: parameters and seed, code commit, data range and version, the lock file.

## Tasks

### 11.1 folds

- Windows ending one day off (start = end − horizon): the check lists the expected windows.
- Newest fold first: the docstring says oldest first.
- `Fold.split` with `<=` on the training side: the first validation day leaks into training.

### 11.2 `backtest`

- One model instance reused across folds: the check detects a model that remembers earlier fits.
- `fit_mask` applied to the validation rows too: the docstring says validation always uses every row of the window. Ask why scoring on in-stock rows is done by `score_forecast`, not by dropping rows.
- `fit_mask` indexed by position instead of by label: misaligned after `fold.split`.

### 11.3 tuning

- One MLflow run for the whole study instead of one per trial: the check counts runs.
- Returning a metric other than the mean in-stock WAPE, or not logging it.
- No seed: results change between runs. Ask how they would reproduce trial 6.
- `mlflow.set_experiment` never called or tracking URI pointing at `./mlruns` in the lab folder: works, but the notebook expects `data/mlflow`.

## The questions at the end of the lab

Discuss, do not grade.

1. **In-stock training flips the bias.** The in-stock model has the better WAPE and forecasts about 4% too much; the all-days model 3% too little. A shop that fears empty shelves more than waste should prefer the in-stock model, which errs on the cheaper side for it.
2. **Where to spend the next week.** Data and features: the training-rows decision gave 0.024, ids as categories 0.04, tuning 0.003. Candidates: better handling of promotions, stockout-aware features, per-segment checks.
3. **Is the best trial's score honest?** No: it was selected as the minimum of 15 noisy measurements on the same folds. An honest estimate comes from data not used for any decision: the locked test week, or an outer backtest.
4. **What MLflow does not record here.** The code commit, the data version and date range, the lock file, the feature list version. MLflow can log them (tags, artifacts); the lab does not.
