## What the learner should own after this module

- Memory follows what you read, so select columns and rows while reading.
- An assumption about data is code that raises, with a count in the message.
- A merge states `on`, `how`, `validate`, and unmatched keys are checked.
- `agg` collapses, `transform` and `shift` keep the shape.
- Lags and windows are computed inside a series, on a sorted complete panel, from past days only.
- A column of arrays becomes one 2-D array; work along an axis instead of looping.
- The processed dataset has a contract that both the writer and the reader check.

Assumed from earlier modules: NumPy axes and masks (05), pandas dtypes, `.loc`, `assign`, method chains (06). If one of these is shaky, that is the thing to explain, not the task.

## Facts about the data worth having at hand

- `train.parquet`: 4.5 million rows, 50,000 series, 90 days, complete, no duplicates, unsorted.
- `discount == 0`: two products have it on nearly every day, sell 10-15 times the average and almost never carry the activity flag; six other products have it on single days, always with `activity_flag == 1`, and sell about three times their usual volume on those days. Free product or placeholder: the data cannot settle it. The course decision is NaN plus the `discount_is_zero` flag.
- Profile `standard` (city 3) shows the "single promo days" pattern: 44 series, 103 rows. Profile `small` (city 14) shows the "always zero" pattern: 7 series of one product, 623 rows. The learner's observation depends on the profile; both are right.
- Discounts above 1 exist only in cities 11 and 15 (34 rows, up to 1.088). No lab profile contains them; the check sample does.
- 8.7% of rows in city 3 have sales recorded in an hour flagged out of stock, 2% of volume.
- Mean sales peak at two to three out-of-stock hours, then fall: products sell out because they sell well.

### 07.1 (a) `shrink_integers`, (b) `load_sales`, (c) `saving`

- Filtering after reading instead of with `filters=`. The check cannot see this; the memory does. Ask: "How much was in memory at the worst moment?"
- Mutating the input in `shrink_integers` (assigning into `df[...]`). The check reports it. Lead to `assign`.
- Downcasting floats too. Ask what `np.float32(0.1) + np.float32(0.2)` prints and what that does to a sum over a million rows.
- Sorting without `reset_index(drop=True)`. Ask: "What does `df.loc[0]` return after the sort?"
- `assign` with a column name held in a variable: `df.assign(**{DATE: ...})`. This unpacking is new to many. Explain it directly; it is syntax, not the point of the task.

### 07.2 (a) five questions, (b) written conclusion, (c) `check_panel`, (d) `clean_discount`

- 07.2a, question 5 (`comparison`) is the hardest exploration step: it needs a merge to restrict `daily` to the series of `zero_days`. If stuck, ask: "Which rows of `daily` do you want to keep, and which table lists them?"
- 07.2b has no check. A good conclusion separates what is certain (where zeros are, how sales behave there), what is not (free product or missing record) and the decision (NaN plus flag).

- Writing one big `if` that reports "invalid data". Ask: "If this fails at 2 a.m., what do you want the message to tell you?"
- Completeness via `nunique` of dates over the whole frame. It misses a series with a hole when other series cover that day. Ask for a frame of two series where one lacks a day.
- `fillna(1.0)` or dropping rows for the zero discount. Go back to the evidence in the notebook and ask what each choice claims to know.
- Capping values above 1. The docstring says to keep them. Ask what a multiplier of 1.05 would mean, and whether it lies on the same scale as 0.9.
- `df["discount"].replace(0, np.nan)` works; `mask` is the idiom the hint suggests. Either is fine.

### 07.3 (a) `build_store_dim`, (b) `build_product_dim`, (c) `attach_dim`, (d) `top_category`

- Building the product dimension with `groupby("product_id").first()`, which silently picks one path when a product has two. The check feeds such a product. Ask: "What should happen if the same product shows up under two categories?"
- Relying on `validate` alone and missing unmatched keys. Point at the widget in the lecture with "brak produktu 92".
- Leaving `_merge` in the result.
- Using `how="inner"` "to be safe". Ask how many rows of the fact table survive when a key is missing.

### 07.4 (a) `load_hourly`, (b) `to_matrix`, (c) `summarise_hours`, (d) `n_disagree`, (e) `share_rows`, `share_sales`

- Looping over rows with `apply`. It passes the check and takes a second instead of milliseconds. After it passes, ask them to time both.
- Off-by-one on the window: `[:, 6:23]` or `[:, 5:22]`. The check compares with the raw column. Ask which array positions are the hours 06:00 to 21:59.
- Summing the status array without `astype(bool)`: works here, since values are 0 and 1. Ask what would happen with a status coded 1 and 2.
- `np.stack(column.to_numpy())` versus `column.tolist()`: both work.

### 07.5 (a) `is_sorted_panel`, (b) `add_lag`, (c) `add_rolling_mean`, (d) `dow_profile`, (e) `corr_lag`, `corr_mean`

- `df[column].shift(lag)` without `groupby`. The check's message names the symptom. Send them to the widget, groupby switched off.
- `groupby(...).rolling(w).mean()` assigned straight back: the index has extra levels and the assignment misaligns or fails. Ask them to print the index of the result.
- Rolling before shifting, or not shifting at all: the window then includes today. The check's message says which number betrays it.
- `dow_profile` without per-series normalisation. The check uses two series of different size and shape to catch it. Ask: "Whose weekend is this profile mostly describing?"
- `is_sorted_panel` by comparing with a sorted copy via `equals`: fine, slower than comparing indices. Do not push the index trick.

### 07.6 (a) `prepare_daily`, (b) `save_processed`, (c) `load_processed`

- Forgetting to select `PROCESSED_COLUMNS` at the end, so the raw stockout column stays.

- `load_processed` that returns whatever is on disk. Ask: "A colleague changed `PROCESSED_COLUMNS` yesterday. What happens when you load your file from last week?"

## The written questions in 07.7

Discuss, do not grade. What a good answer contains:

1. **Rising sales.** Two explanations: a real upward trend (growth of the business, more customers), and season (temperature rises from 17 to 27 °C over the same 90 days). Ninety days cannot separate them: there is no second spring to compare with.
2. **Relative sales by discount.** Raw sales mix two effects: what a discount does, and which products get discounted. If slow sellers are discounted more often, raw sales under discount look low even if every discount helps. Dividing by the series mean compares each series with itself.
3. **Two to three out-of-stock hours beat zero.** Reverse causation: the product ran out because it sold well. Stockout hours are partly a consequence of demand.
4. **A model trained on all rows.** It learns the average of censored and uncensored days, so on a fully stocked day it predicts too little. It underestimates demand.
